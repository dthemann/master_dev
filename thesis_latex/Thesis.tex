\outline{Molecular docking predicts how small-molecule ligands bind their protein
targets, yet physics-based and machine-learning tools embody very different
trade-offs between geometric accuracy and physical realism. This thesis presents a
systematic, provenance-documented comparison of a physics-based method (AutoDock Vina)
against two artificial-intelligence (AI)-based methods, namely the diffusion-based
generative model DiffDock-L (hereafter DiffDock) and the
E(3)-equivariant regression model EquiBind, across two complementary settings. A
calibration benchmark of 303 co-crystallised complexes enables ground-truth
assessment of both pose accuracy (heavy-atom root-mean-square deviation, RMSD) and physical validity through the
PoseBusters test battery. Because the dominant variant of each toolchain is both chosen and scored on
this same benchmark, the reported rates are optimistic as estimates of performance
on unseen targets. A crystal-free application to the calcium-release-activated
channel Orai1, using molecular-dynamics receptor snapshots together with experimental
and benchmark ligand sets, probes how the tools behave on a realistic
membrane-protein target without a reference structure.

Across both settings, physical validity separates the tools most sharply in
their raw output. On the calibration benchmark, adding the validity requirement to
the near-native recovery criterion costs the refined pipelines at most 1.3
percentage points of complexes, so placement and pose selection rather than
validity become the limiting factors on that endpoint. At the level of individual
poses the gate remains costly even after minimisation, removing 15.5\% of refined
DiffDock poses and 48.2\% of refined EquiBind poses. Pooled over every produced pose, raw AutoDock Vina is physically valid
almost everywhere (98.2\%), whereas the raw AI outputs are largely physically invalid
(24.4\% for DiffDock and 2.9\% for unguided EquiBind), and AutoDock Vina followed
by gnina rescoring reaches 99.5\% on that same pooled basis. On the separate basis of the median per-complex validity rate,
post-hoc minimisation is the dominant driver of usability, raising DiffDock from
13.3\% to about 93\% with smina and unguided EquiBind from 0\% to about 57\% with
gnina, while it cannot recover an unsampled or grossly misplaced binding mode. Deepening the inspected ranking pool from rank-1 to the best of fifteen
recovers valid, near-native poses for a further 21.1 percentage points of
complexes with DiffDock and 20.5 with AutoDock Vina but only 5.0 with EquiBind,
and extending it to thirty adds at most 0.7 points more, so the headroom is
large, unequally distributed and almost exhausted by a top-15 pool. On Orai1 the three tools localise to markedly
different sites, and there the combined screen for physical validity and
admissible placement rather than validity alone decides which poses remain
usable. Transmembrane-pore filtering, pose clustering and protein--ligand
interaction-fingerprint analysis quantify their disagreement.
Overall, the results argue for combined accuracy-and-validity criteria and for
geometry refinement as a default stage when AI-based docking tools of this
generation are deployed, with the choice of refinement itself validated, since one
relaxation stage tested here damaged the geometry it was meant to repair.}

\keywords{Molecular docking; Physics-based versus AI-based methods; PoseBusters
physical validity; Post-hoc pose refinement; Orai1 calcium channel}
